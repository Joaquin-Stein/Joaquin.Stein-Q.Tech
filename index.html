Task 4 — Insights & Recommendations

Findings & Next Steps

Three key findings, two concrete recommendations, and the one question this dataset cannot answer.

Key Findings

1 — Science & Future Tech wins on CTR. Engineering & Construction wins on retention.

Science & Future Tech has the highest average CTR (4.76%) — these videos have more clickable thumbnails and titles that spark curiosity. Engineering & Construction has the highest average retention (47.9%) — viewers stay longest on large-scale build and infrastructure content. DIY & Self-Build sits below both categories on both metrics, though individual videos like QTH006 outperform the group average significantly.

2 — Hyperloop and 3D Printer are the channel's most well-rounded performers.

Most videos are strong in one area but not another. Hyperloop and World's Largest 3D Printer score well across every metric simultaneously: high total views, high CTR, a growing trend, and solid retention. These two represent the content formula that works best for this channel right now.

3 — Building an Underground House is invisible to the algorithm — not to viewers.

QTH006 averages ~5K views/day vs the channel average of ~42K. Yet viewers who find it watch 51% of a 28-minute video — the joint-highest retention in the dataset. The problem is discoverability, not quality. This video is being underserved by the algorithm and deserves direct attention.

Recommendations

Rec. 1 — Run a thumbnail test on QTH006

Given strong retention but poor impressions, the most likely fix is the thumbnail and title. The algorithm surfaces videos that get clicked — if the thumbnail is not generating clicks, the video never gets the chance to prove itself.

Action: Run a thumbnail A/B test on QTH006, similar to the tests already run on other videos. A video with 51% retention on a 28-minute video is a genuine asset. It just needs to be found.

Rec. 2 — Use QTH010 and QTH003 as the content template

Both videos combine a large-scale subject, a visually compelling title, and strong real-world footage. They perform well across every metric the channel tracks.

Action: Benchmark future video concepts against these two before production. The data shows the audience responds best to content that is both spectacular and educational — not one or the other.

The question this dataset cannot answer

Where exactly are viewers dropping off within each video?

We know what percentage of a video is watched on average — but not where viewers stop watching. A 50% retention rate could mean everyone watches exactly halfway through, or it could mean half the viewers finish it completely and the other half leave in the first 30 seconds. These are two very different problems with very different solutions.

Access to the audience retention curve from YouTube Studio for each video would allow the team to identify whether drop-offs happen in the intro, middle, or ending — enabling targeted improvements to content structure rather than guesswork.
